Key assumptions Landlords pass 50% of property tax to renters. That is the share ITEP's Who Pays? assumes, calling it roughly the midpoint of the best available research, and the benchmark these results are checked against. Estimates range widely: Carroll and Yinger (1994) found 10% to 15%; a 2020 Census Bureau working paper by Schwegman and Yinger found 14% to 27%, while the older studies it reviews found 50% to 110%. The newest, a 2025 Philadelphia Fed working paper by Baker, finds 50% to 89% for rentals in Berkeley, Calif. Each state's detail panel also shows 15% and 100%. Home value and rent scale with income at each state's median ratio. This is a simplification: research finds housing spending rises more slowly than income (Davis and Ortalo-Magné; Goodman and Kawai). The model therefore likely overstates property tax for high-income families and understates it for low-income families, making property tax look less regressive than it is. One conventional mortgage: 80% loan-to-value at the 2025 average 30-year fixed rate from Freddie Mac's mortgage rate survey, which assumes 20% down.
